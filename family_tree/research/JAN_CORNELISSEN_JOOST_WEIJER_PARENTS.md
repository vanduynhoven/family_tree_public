# Parents of Jan Cornelissen (@I422@) and Joost van de Weijer (@I424@)

**Research date:** 2026-08-30
**Source:** OpenArchieven API (archive: BHIC / Brabants Historisch Informatie Centrum)
**Goal:** Identify the parents (great-grandparents in our tree) of:
- **Jan Cornelissen** (@I422@, ~1780, Bakel) × Wilhelmina van de Meulenhof (@I423@)
  → parents of Cornelis Cornelissen (@I418@, bap. 1808 Bakel)
- **Joost van de Weijer** (@I424@, ~1775, Bakel) × Josina Biemans (@I425@)
  → parents of Ida van de Weijer (@I419@, d. 1842 Bakel)

## Conclusion

**PARENTS NOT FOUND.** Neither Jan Cornelissen's nor Joost van de Weijer's own
parents could be documented from OpenArchieven/BHIC. The generation-defining
records (their baptisms, or their own marriage records, which would name their
parents) are **not indexed** in this source. Bakel's DTB Dopen (baptism) register
contains essentially no matching baptisms for either man in the relevant window
(1750–1790): only 4 Cornelissen and 1 van de Weijer baptism entries exist for all
of Bakel en Milheeze in that period, none of them Jan or Joost. This strongly
suggests both men were **born outside Bakel** (or in a gap in Bakel's DTB
coverage) and married into / migrated to the parish.

The couples themselves (@I422@×@I423@ and @I424@×@I425@) were, however,
**independently corroborated** by multiple civil-registration records (see below),
so the existing tree entries are sound — only the fourth generation up is blocked.

## What WAS confirmed (corroborating the target couples)

### Jan Cornelissen × Wilhelmina van de Meulenhof (@I422@ × @I423@)
- **Death of Wilhelmina, 12 June 1811, Bakel en Milheeze** (BS Overlijden)
  `bhi:1158c7a0-285f-b873-02d5-2ccbd0029a3d`
  Overledene: *Guilhelmine Meulenhof*. Her own parents recorded as **N.N.**
  (unknown) — so this record does not extend her line either.
- **Marriage 7 May 1815, Bakel en Milheeze** (BS Huwelijk)
  `bhi:5154a458-01dd-da70-6ccb-9746ebbd49ed`
  Groom *Theodorus Cornelissen* (b. Bakel); **Vader: Joannes Cornelissen**,
  **Moeder: Willemijn Meulenhof** → confirms Jan (Joannes) Cornelissen ×
  Wilhelmina/Willemijn Meulenhof as a couple with children in Bakel.
  (Name forms seen: Jan = Joannes = Jean; Wilhelmina = Willemijn = Guilhelmine.)

### Joost van de Weijer × Josina Biemans (@I424@ × @I425@)
- **Death of Ida, 21 Jan 1842, Bakel en Milheeze** (BS Overlijden)
  `bhi:3909870a-2c03-5c99-c400-75564c21a955`
  Overledene: *Ida Weijer*; **Vader: Joost Weijer**, **Moeder: Josina Biemans**
  → directly confirms Ida van de Weijer's parents = Joost van de Weijer ×
  Josina Biemans. (Joost's/Josina's OWN parents are not on this record.)
- **Marriage 15 Oct 1812, Bakel en Milheeze** (BS Huwelijk)
  `bhi:9c8468b3-7c70-2b93-e5d3-ab94d6791235`
  Bride *Jeannette Weijer* (b. Bakel); **Vader: Jacques Weijer**,
  **Moeder: Justinienne Biemans** → a second, independent record of the couple
  **Joost/Jacques van de Weijer × Josina/Justinienne Biemans** with another
  daughter (Jeannette). Confirms name variants: **Joost = Jacques = Judocus**;
  **Josina = Justinienne = Justina**.

### Also cross-confirmed (child of Cornelis × Ida)
- Marriage 20 Apr 1833, Bakel `bhi:ed1ecde7-3191-66bb-28c2-546ed7e13e0e`:
  groom Theodorus Cornelissen, **Vader: Cornelis Cornelissen, Moeder: Ida Weijer**.
- Marriage 30 Jan 1841, Bakel `bhi:1e1a1e8b-93a0-b283-8aa3-89b348420491`:
  bride Johanna Cornelissen, **Vader: Cornelis Cornelissen, Moeder: Ida Weijer**.
  These confirm Cornelis Cornelissen (@I418@) × Ida van de Weijer (@I419@) as
  the parent generation linking the two families.

## Searches that returned NOTHING (the dead-ends)

All queries below used `archive_code=bhi` and, where noted, `eventplace=Bakel en Milheeze`:

| Query | Result |
|---|---|
| Jan/Joannes Cornelissen — DTB Trouwen, Bakel | 0 |
| Jan/Joannes Cornelissen — BS Overlijden *Overledene*, Bakel 1811–1820 | 0 |
| Cornelissen — DTB Dopen, Bakel 1750–1785 | 4 (none = Jan) |
| Weijer/Jacobus Weijer — BS Overlijden *Overledene*, Bakel | only a *different* Joost, d. 1874 (parents Johannes Weijer × Antonet Hout — a younger namesake) |
| Weijer — DTB Dopen, Bakel (all years) | 1 (a witness, 1794) |
| Weijer & Biemans — DTB Trouwen / BS Huwelijk (their own marriage) | 0 matching Joost×Josina marriage record |
| Cornelis Cornelissen / Ida Weijer — own marriage record | **not indexed** (Ida never appears as "Bruid"; only ever as mother-of-bride/groom) |

The couples' **own marriage records** — which by Dutch civil/church convention
name each spouse's parents — are simply absent from the indexed data. Cornelis ×
Ida married before ~1826 (the earliest indexed Bakel BS Huwelijk that names them
is 1833, as parents), i.e. in the pre-1811 DTB era or an unindexed early-BS gap.

## Leads for future research (outside OpenArchieven)

1. **Find Jan Cornelissen's & Joost van de Weijer's places of birth first.** Both
   were almost certainly born outside Bakel. Neighbouring parishes to check:
   Aarle-Rixtel (a *Judocus van de Weijer* baptism appears there 5 Nov 1802 —
   too late for our Joost but shows the surname is native to Aarle-Rixtel),
   Deurne, Gemert, Beek en Donk, Lieshout.
2. **Locate the Cornelis Cornelissen × Ida van de Weijer marriage record**
   (~1805–1826). Once found, it will name BOTH sets of parents-of-the-spouses,
   i.e. Jan Cornelissen's parents and Joost van de Weijer's parents, and typically
   states each grandparent's residence/whether deceased — the single highest-value
   missing document. Check the BHIC studiezaal / genealogy portal and RHCe/
   Regionaal Historisch Centrum Eindhoven for Bakel DTB Trouwen 1800–1811 and
   the earliest BS Huwelijk 1811–1826.
3. **Note the patronymic clue:** in the 1815 infant-death record
   `bhi:59b112dc-...` a "Cornelis **Jan** Cornelissen" appears — the pattern
   *Cornelis, son of Jan* is consistent with Jan's surname "Cornelissen" itself
   being a patronymic (Jan, son of a **Cornelis**), a common trap; do not assume
   Jan's father is named Cornelis without the marriage record.
