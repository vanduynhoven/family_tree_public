## SUMMARY

The Van Duijnhoven family tree reveals a dense endogamous network spanning **1754–1946**,
concentrated in the Catholic rural communities of Uden, Bakel en Milheeze, and surrounding
Noord-Brabant villages. At least **12 confirmed endogamous connections** link the family
to a handful of recurring surnames: **Verwegen**, **Van der Wijst**, **Biemans**, and **Wassenberg**.

### B. Van Duijnhoven × Van der Wijst Alliance (5 connections, 2 generations)

The second-largest endogamy cluster. The van der Wijst family entered the VD network
through the Verwegen connection and remained for 80 years.

| Year | Connection | Notes |

| **1863** | Adrianus Verwegen × **Everdina van der Wijst** | Geertruda's brother married a van der Wijst |

Lambertus). The same pattern repeated across two generations.

### D. The Van Leuken Pedigree Collapse (Anna Maria van den Elzen)

A structural pedigree collapse confirmed in the maternal line of Anna Maria van den Elzen
(Gen 3 wife of Marianus van Duijnhoven):

**Peter van Leuken × Jenneke Jan Bankers** (Boekel) had two daughters:
- **Joanna Maria van Leuken** → married Dirck Cornelis van den Elsen (1768 Boekel)
  → Cornelius van den Elsen → Gerardus Cornelus → Johannes van den Elzen → **Anna Maria** (paternal)
- **Cornelia van Leuken** → married Hubertus Dapperen (1800 Boekel)
  → Ida Dapperen → Hubertus van der Horst → Francijna → Johannes van den Elzen → **Anna Maria** (maternal)

**Result:** Anna Maria van den Elzen descends from Peter van Leuken **twice** (via both her
father's and mother's lines), making her nominally more related to him than a standard
5×-great-grandchild.

## STRUCTURAL OBSERVATION

The Van Duijnhoven family lived in a cluster of Catholic villages (Uden, Bakel, Boekel, Zeeland NB,
Erp, Stiphout) where families repeatedly married into each other across generations. The recurring
surnames — Verwegen, van der Wijst, Biemans, Wassenberg — form a tight web in which the Van
Duijnhoven family is both a hub and participant.

The endogamy is not unusual for 19th-century Catholic rural Brabant, where:
- The parish was the social unit
- Land inheritance favored local marriage
- The Church's consanguinity rules required dispensations but did not prevent cousin marriages
- The same families appear as godparents, witnesses, and spouses across 3-4 generations

The **5-generation VD×Verwegen alliance** (1856–1922) and the **two sibling swaps** in the
van der Wijst network are particularly striking examples of deliberate family alliance strategies.
