### Green Isle, Minnesota - Historical Context

**Source:** Wikipedia - Green Isle, Minnesota  
**URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Isle,_Minnesota

**Key Context:**
- Green Isle was platted in 1881
- Named after the township, which alludes to Ireland (Emerald Isle)
- **Most early settlers were from Ireland**, not the Netherlands
- Located in Sibley County

**Implication:** If the Van Duynhoven family settled in Green Isle, they would have been among a minority of Dutch settlers in a predominantly Irish community. This may explain limited newspaper coverage - the family may not have been prominent in local affairs dominated by Irish families.

### Dutch Settlement in Minnesota

**Source:** Minnesota Historical Society Magazine  
**URL:** http://collections.mnhs.org/MNHistoryMagazine/articles/53/v53i06p214-224.pdf

**Key Excerpt (from search snippet):**
> "Dutch settlers were not among the first wave of immigrants to Minnesota's prairies, nor were they brand-new Americans desperate for a fresh start. They were, for the most part, cash-poor but experienced farmers who were lured to Minnesota in the last decades of the nineteenth century from well-established midwestern colonies. They were promised cheap, fertile land and communities where the old language would be spoken on the street, the old foods found on the table, and the old religion observed in church."

> "Minnesota's Dutch settled along a 250-mile arc from the southwest corner of the state toward Duluth in the northeast in more than a dozen towns still marked by their 'Dutchness.' Just north of Pipestone is the village of Holland. In 1960 the Minnesota state boys basketball champions were Edgerton's Flying Dutchmen."

**Dutch Settlement Areas in Minnesota:**
- Holland (near Pipestone)
- Edgerton
- Clara City (Niederland Township)
- Greenleafton

**Note:** Green Isle is NOT listed among the traditional Dutch settlement areas - it was Irish territory. The Van Duynhoven family's presence there may have been unusual.
