### Ortscheid Surname Research

**Geographic Origin (Geneanet):**
- Surname ORTSCHEID is documented in 1,990 individuals in genealogical databases
- Most common in Rhineland-Palatinate region of Germany
- Related spellings: ORTSCHEIT, ORTSCHEIDT, ORTSCHEITT, ORSCHEIDT, ORSCHEID
- Etymology: likely from German "Ort" (place/point) + "scheid" (from Wasserscheide/watershed boundary)
- Village of Scheid exists in Rhineland-Palatinate

**US Distribution (Ancestry):**
- Found in USA 1880-1920
- In 1880: 14 Ortscheid families living in Illinois (61% of all US Ortscheids)
- Illinois was the primary Ortscheid settlement center
- Secondary locations: Wisconsin

**Key Finding - John Baptist Ortscheid (Geneanet):**
- Birth: 1842
- Death: 1931
- Spouse: Catherine Zeig (married 1864)
- Location: appears in family tree maintained by user "srichardson"
- **This may be Joseph's father (Gen 3) or grandfather (Gen 4)**

## McDONALD LINE (Teresa's Parents)

### Generation 4-8: McDonald Irish Ancestry

**McDonald Surname:**
- Anglicized form of Scottish Gaelic "Mac Dhòmhnaill" (son of Donald)
- Also common Irish variant "MacDonald"
- One of the most populous surnames in both Scotland and Ireland

**Wisconsin Irish Settlement Context:**
Per MNopedia research on Goodhue County (similar patterns in Grant County):
- Irish immigrants arrived in Wisconsin from 1840s onward
- Many were Famine refugees (1845-1852)
- Established Catholic parishes as community centers
- Often worked as railroad laborers before settling on farms

**Cuba City Irish Context:**
- St. Rose of Lima Catholic Church (founded 1851) served Irish and German Catholics
- The parish was established by Fr. Samuel Mazzuchelli, an Italian Dominican who specifically ministered to immigrant communities
- Irish and German intermarriage was common in second generation

**HYPOTHESIS - McDonald Ancestry:**

**Generation 4 (Teresa's Parents, Great-Great-Grandparents of Mary Cecilia):**
- Father: Unknown McDonald (b. ~1855-1875, likely Wisconsin or Ireland)
- Mother: Unknown (Irish surname likely)

**Generation 5:**
- Likely Irish immigrants who arrived during or shortly after the Great Famine (1845-1852)
- May have entered via New York, Boston, or New Orleans
- Probably from Counties Mayo, Galway, Clare, or Cork (major emigration sources)

**Generations 6-8:**
- Would require Irish parish records from County of origin
- Pre-Famine Irish records are sparse outside Church registers

### Generation 3: Parents of Genevieve Campbell

**Research Context - Irish in Goodhue County:**
Per MNopedia:
> "By 1880, Goodhue County held within its borders four significant Euro-American immigrant enclaves: Minnesota's largest group of Swedes; the second largest assembly of Norwegians; one of the most densely populated German tracts; and **an Irish colony at the county's center** [Belle Creek Township]."

The Irish settlement in Goodhue County dates to 1854:
> "Railroad workers and friends Walter Doyle and James O'Neill decided to take their families to Minnesota in 1854. Landing at Red Wing, they walked south and west, looking for land to claim. They found it in Belle Creek Township. In 1860, as the community grew, Irish newcomers built the first St. Columbkill church."

**HYPOTHESIS - Campbell Irish Origin:**

Campbell is a Scottish/Irish surname meaning "crooked mouth" (Gaelic: Caimbeul).

Given:
- Catholic burial (Calvary Cemetery)
- Location in area with established Irish community
- Timeline (Genevieve b. 1882 = parents likely immigrants ~1850-1870)

**Generation 3 (Genevieve's Parents, Great-Grandparents of Fairman):**

  - Likely Irish or Scottish immigrant

  - The name "Fairman" might be her maiden name

### Generation 4-8: Campbell Deep Ancestry
