## D. HISTORICAL CONTEXT FOR GENERATION 2 (1829–1903)

**Martinus lived through:**
- **1829**: Born during the reign of King William I of the Netherlands
- **1830**: Belgian Revolution (Belgium separates from Netherlands)  
- **1839**: Treaty of London (Belgium recognized as independent)
- **1848**: Dutch Constitutional Reform (King William II grants more democratic rights)
- **1853**: Restoration of Catholic Church hierarchy in the Netherlands (ending ~250 years of suppression)
- **1860s–70s**: Railroad era begins in Noord-Brabant
- **1880s**: Agricultural crisis affects Dutch farming communities
- **1903**: Died in Uden, having seen the Netherlands transform from a feudal to a modern state

**Catholic emancipation (1853)** was particularly significant — Martinus was 24 when the Catholic Church was officially restored in the Netherlands. This allowed openly Catholic naming traditions to flourish again; his son was named **Marianus** (of Mary), a distinctly Catholic devotional name.

**Farming life in Uden:** Martinus almost certainly worked as a farmer (akkerbouwer) like the neighboring collateral branch Martinus (1828–1890) who was explicitly documented as an akkerbouwer. Uden in this era was an agricultural village in sandy Noord-Brabant.
