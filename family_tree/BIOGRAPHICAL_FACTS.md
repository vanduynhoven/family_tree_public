## 1. Harry James Duynhoven, QSO — New Zealand politician

1. Born **22 June 1955 in New Plymouth, New Zealand**; he is a member of the New Zealand **Labour Party**. His father was **from the Netherlands**, giving him Dutch descent (Wikipedia categorises him under "New Zealand people of Dutch descent").
2. **Member of Parliament for the New Plymouth electorate** across three spans: **1987–1990, 1993–2003, and 2003–2008** (42nd, 44th–48th Parliaments). He first won the seat from National's Tony Friedlander in 1987 and lost it in 2008 to National's Jonathan Young by just **105 votes** — the smallest margin of that election.
3. Served as a **Minister outside Cabinet** in Helen Clark's Fifth Labour Government, holding **Associate Minister of Transport**, then **Minister for Transport Safety** and **Associate Minister of Energy**, until the government's 2008 defeat.
4. **2003 citizenship affair:** after he applied to *resume* his Dutch citizenship (held from birth but lost via a Dutch law change), the Privileges Committee ruled his seat vacated under electoral law. The government passed a **retroactive amending act** so he could keep his seat — a notable episode in NZ electoral/constitutional history.
5. Appointed a **Companion of the Queen's Service Order (QSO)** in the **2012 New Year Honours** for services as an MP; earlier awarded the **New Zealand 1990 Commemoration Medal**. Left Parliament at age 16 originally to train as an **electrician**, later a technical teacher.
6. **Local government:** elected **Mayor of New Plymouth (2010–2013)**, then returned as a **district councillor** via a 2015 by-election (re-elected 2016 & 2019, also elected to the Taranaki District Health Board); announced retirement from council at the 2025 local elections.

## 2. Albert Van Dyn Hoven — Wisconsin founder (born ~1875, Netherlands)

**No direct, verifiable biographical record was found** for an individual named "Albert Van Dyn Hoven" in the searched public sources (Wikipedia, university/historical-society pages, Little Chute Historical Society). Below are **verified contextual facts** about the Dutch-Catholic community of Little Chute / Fox Valley that such a person would have belonged to. The individual-level claims should be confirmed against primary genealogy records (census, church, ship manifests) before being treated as established.

1. Little Chute, Wisconsin (Outagamie County, along the **Fox River**, just east of Appleton) was settled in the late 19th century by **Dutch Catholic immigrants from North Brabant**, and became the **largest Catholic Dutch community in the United States** by the early 20th century.
2. The settlement was initiated by the **Dominican missionary Father Theodore J. van den Broek**, who led the first Dutch Catholics from the **Uden** area to Little Chute in **1848**; immigration from that region of the Netherlands continued **into the early 20th century** (i.e., through the ~1875-born generation).
3. Most early emigrants came from **villages near Uden** — including Zeeland, Boekel, Mill, Oploo and Gemert (North Brabant) — and were **primarily farmers** who chose the Midwest because its landscape allowed them to continue the **same agricultural practices** (later including **dairy**, the region's signature farming).
4. **St. John Nepomucene Church** was the community's religious and educational focal point from its founding and remains a thriving parish; the area retained the Dutch language ("speaking Hollander") and customs such as **Sinterklaas / "St. Nick's Day" (Dec 6)** into the 20th century.
5. Little Chute was formally **incorporated as a village on March 8, 1899**; by 1927 an estimated **40,000 Dutch Roman Catholics** had immigrated to the U.S., concentrated most heavily in this Fox Valley area. (A nearby town, **Vandenbroek, Wisconsin**, is named for the founding priest.)

## 3. Deacon Mark van Duynhoven — Annandale, NJ (Diocese of Metuchen)

1. **Ordained a permanent deacon** by **Bishop James Francis Checchio on May 20, 2017**, in a class of **15 men** ordained for the Diocese of Metuchen at the **Cathedral of St. Francis of Assisi**. Serves at **Immaculate Conception Parish, Annandale, NJ**.
2. He and his wife **Michele** have lived in Annandale and been members of Immaculate Conception **since 1996**. They have two daughters — **Carol** (Bucks County, PA) and **Sarah** (Hoboken, NJ).
3. Holds a **Master of Arts in Theology** and an **MBA**, both from **Seton Hall University**, and a **Bachelor of Science in Chemistry** from **Siena College**.
4. Spent his **entire career in Quality Assurance and Supply Chain** roles within **medical device and pharmaceutical** businesses.
5. **Ministry:** currently volunteers with **Hunterdon Hospice** and leads the parish's **Rite of Christian Initiation of Adults (RCIA)** preparation/formation program.
6. **Family link (cross-verified):** He is a **son of Joseph Cornelius van Duynhoven** (see #4) — Joseph's 2019 obituary lists "Mark van Duynhoven and his wife Michele of Annandale, NJ" among the survivors.

## 4. Joseph Cornelius van Duynhoven (1928 Halfweg, NL – 2019 Bellvale, NY)

1. **Born February 12, 1928 in Halfweg, The Netherlands**, the son of **John van Duynhoven and Marie Tol**; died **November 27, 2019, aged 91, in Bellvale, NY** (Orange County), surrounded by his wife and children.
2. **Served in the U.S. Army from 1954–1956**, confirming his emigration to the United States by the mid-1950s.
3. Married **Cornelia ("Corry") Alders on February 12, 1958**; the marriage lasted **61 years**. They had children Casey, Mark (the deacon, see #3), and Marie, plus **10 grandchildren and 2 great-grandchildren**.
4. A **master woodworker**: he and Corry were known for handcrafted gifts — birdhouses, feeders, and especially **Christmas crèches**, one of which he made and donated to his church.
5. **Community & faith:** a member of **St. Stephen the Martyr Parish** and of the **Warwick Golden Seniors**; in retirement he and Corry travelled widely (multiple cross-country U.S. drives, cruises, and a **pilgrimage to Ireland**). Funeral Mass was held at St. Stephen Church, Warwick, NY.

**Sources:**

Verified **contextual facts** about the Van Duijnhoven family's home town of Uden follow:

1. **Uden** is a town in the province of **North Brabant, Netherlands** (part of the new municipality of Maashorst since 2022); the **Van Duijnhoven / Van Duynhoven surname is documented in Uden's civil and population registers** across the 19th and early 20th centuries (e.g., openarchieven.nl and genealogieonline.nl hold multiple Uden Van Duijnhoven records).
2. Uden was historically a **Catholic haven** — it lay outside the Dutch Republic after 1648 and was a place of religious tolerance where surrounding Catholics built churches; the **Church of St. Peter (Sint-Petrus)** and monastic orders (Crosiers, Ursulines, Brigittines) anchored its Catholic life. A Catholic farming family in Uden c. 1900 is entirely consistent with the town's character.
3. From **1848 onward**, economic stagnation drove **emigration from Uden and its neighbouring Brabant villages to the American Midwest** (Little Chute, Wisconsin), a pattern that continued into the early 20th century — the same migration stream that connects the Uden-area Van Duijnhovens to the U.S. branches.
4. The Uden-area population were **primarily farmers**; the town specialised in **cherry growing from 1860** and cultivated the eastern heathlands ("De Peel") in the 1920s — the agricultural context in which a Brabant farming family of that era lived.
5. Large Catholic families were the regional norm; while the specific "12 children" claim for this Marianus is unverified online, big families were typical of the North-Brabant Catholic farming communities of the late-1800s / early-1900s.
