## 1. Summary & the Netherlands connection

The Wisconsin "Van Dyn Hoven" family is concentrated in the **Fox River villages of Little Chute and Kaukauna**, in Outagamie County. This community has a **documented origin directly in Uden, North Brabant** — the same town from which Arthur van Duynhoven's paternal line originates.

**The Little Chute founding fact (well documented):**
- Little Chute, WI was settled in **1848** by a group of Dutch Catholics led by the Dominican missionary **Father Theodore J. van den Broek**, who came **from Uden, North Brabant, Netherlands**.
- Father van den Broek returned to the Netherlands in 1847 and brought back the first Dutch settlers in 1848, beginning a wave of North Brabant → northeast Wisconsin emigration that continued into the early 20th century.
- The village retains its Dutch-Catholic identity (St. John Nepomucene Catholic Church; an operational Dutch windmill shipped from the Netherlands in 2011).

**Why this matters for Arthur's tree:**
- Arthur's grandfather **Johan van Duijnhoven (b. 12 Nov 1915, Uden)** descends from the **Uden van Duijnhoven line** (Marianus 1872–1949 → Martinus 1829–1903 → Petrus 1799–1882).
- The Wisconsin Van Dyn Hovens descend from immigrants who left **the exact same district (Uden / North Brabant)**, one to three generations *earlier* (mid-to-late 1800s), during the van den Broek emigration wave. A littlechutehistory.org settler biography explicitly records a **"Van Duijnhoven"** family in Uden feeding the Little Chute migration.
- **Connection status: HIGHLY PLAUSIBLE but NOT YET PROVEN.** Same surname root, same origin village, same emigration channel. The missing link is the immigrant ancestor (the father/grandfather of **Albert Van Dyn Hoven**, below) and his Uden baptism record. If that man ties back to Petrus van Duijnhoven (1799–1882) or a sibling line, Arthur and the Wisconsin family are cousins. **See §5 Open Questions.**
