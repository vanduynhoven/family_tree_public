## 8. Dutch Immigration Context (Little Chute, Wisconsin)

### Historical Background
- **First Settlement:** 1848
- **Leader:** Father Theodore J. van den Broek (Dominican priest from Uden, Netherlands)
  - Born: Amsterdam, November 5, 1783
  - Died: Little Chute, November 5, 1851
  - Advertised Wisconsin in Netherlands
- **Origin:** Primarily North Brabant, Netherlands (especially Uden)
- **Religion:** Dutch Catholic

### Immigration Pattern
- 1848: First wave led by Father van den Broek
- Immigration continued until at least 1960
- Part of larger Dutch immigration: Michigan (30,406), Illinois (21,916), New Jersey (10,261), Iowa (9,388), New York (9,414), Wisconsin (6,496) as of 1919 records

### Cultural Markers Today
- Dutch windmill in downtown Little Chute (tourist attraction/community center)
- *Schut* festival in Hollandtown (since 1849)
- Dutch words in local English: *oma*, *opa*, *tante*
- Dutch food terms: *stamppot*, *potassi*, *wortelstoemp*, *brij*

- UW-Madison Wisconsin Languages: https://wep.csumc.wisc.edu/dutch-in-wisconsin/
