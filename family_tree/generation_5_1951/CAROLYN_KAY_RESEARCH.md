## Analysis & Theories

### Theory 1: Infant/Child Death (MOST LIKELY)
Carolyn Kay may have died in infancy or early childhood (1957-1963), similar to her younger sister Mary C (1959-1963). The complete absence from:
- FindAGrave (no memorial exists)
- Online obituary searches
- Minnesota Death Index web searches
- St. Brendan's Cemetery public records

...suggests either:
- She is buried in an unmarked grave or a grave not yet indexed on FindAGrave
- She died elsewhere (not in Sibley County)
- Records were never digitized or indexed

### Theory 2: Living Person
Carolyn Kay could still be alive (would be ~69 years old in 2026). The lack of death records could simply mean she:
- Married and changed her surname
- Moved out of Minnesota
- Has minimal online presence

However, she is not mentioned in Peter John Van Duynhoven's June 2026 obituary, which typically lists surviving siblings.

### Theory 3: Not the Same Family
There is a possibility this is a different John Van Duynhoven/Annamarie combination, though this is unlikely given:
- Exact location match (Sibley Township)
- Birth year fits the known child-bearing pattern (1951, 1953, 1954, 1956, **1957**, 1959)

### Theory 4: Alternative Name for Anna Maria
Some Catholic families used different names for baptism vs. civil records. However:
- Anna Maria was born 19 Dec 1954 (confirmed)
- Carolyn Kay was born 11 Jul 1957
- These are clearly different individuals (2.5 years apart)
