# Early Van Duijnhoven Ancestors — 1600s–1700s

**Scope:** The oldest confirmed and inferred generations of the paternal Van Duijnhoven
line in **Uden** (Noord-Brabant, Meierij van 's-Hertogenbosch), pushing back from the
previously-known couple of Generation −1 into the pre-1811 Roman-Catholic DTB
(church) registers.

**Primary source:** Open Archieven / Brabants Historisch Informatie Centrum (BHIC),
DTB Dopen (baptisms) and DTB Trouwen (marriages), searched via the public JSON API
(`api.openarchieven.nl`). All records are Roman-Catholic parish registers of Uden.

**Spelling note:** In the Dutch registers the name is uniformly **van Duijnhoven** (ij
digraph). The US branch later spells it **van Duynhoven** (y). Search both.

> ⚠️ **Reconciliation with prior notes.** Earlier project notes listed the base couple
> as "Petrus Joannis van Duijnhoven married Elisabeth van Boxtel (1808 Uden)." The
> actual DTB marriage record is **21 November 1796 in Uden** (see G-1 below), and a
> 1799 baptism shows Petrus was **already deceased by August 1799**. Any "1808" date
> is therefore a later civil act or a remarriage of the widow, **not** this couple's
> marriage. The 1796 record is the authoritative one.

## Generation −3 — Inferred earliest Uden ancestor (patronymic evidence)

### Petrus (Peeters) van Duijnhoven — *active c. 1720s–1740s, Uden region*

**Not yet located in a direct record**, but firmly implied by the patronym chain:

```
Petrus van Duijnhoven          (Gen −3, father named "Peeters/Petri")
        │
        └── Joannes Petri van Duijnhoven   (Gen −2, b. ~1730s; m. Aldegundis Jansen)
                    │
                    └── Petrus Joannis van Duijnhoven  (Gen −1, b. ~1760–1770 Uden; m. Elisabeth van Boxtel 1796)
                                │
                                └── Petrus van Duijnhoven  (bapt. 27 Aug 1799 Uden)
```

The name **alternates Petrus → Joannes → Petrus → Petrus** across the generations —
classic Dutch Catholic naming (a son named for his grandfather). Gen −3 Petrus would
have been born **c. 1700–1710** and married **c. 1725–1735**. His baptism/marriage
would fall in the **pre-1755 Uden books or a neighbouring parish** (Boekel, Gemert,
Volkel, Zeeland, Erp), which are the next records to pull.

## The 1762–1782 Uden baptism gap

## Surname origin — where does "van Duijnhoven" come from?

**Type:** *Toponymic* (place-based) surname, formed with the preposition **van** ("of /
from") + the place-element **Duijnhoven**. It marks a family that once lived at, or came
from, a farm/hamlet of that name — not a patronymic and not an occupational name.

**Etymology of "Duijnhoven":** a compound of
- **duin / duijn** — a sandy rise or dune-like elevation of dry, sandy ground (the older
  Brabant sense is an inland sandy hillock on the heath, not a coastal dune), and
- **-hoven** — plural of **hof** = "farmstead(s), courts, homesteads."

So **Duijnhoven ≈ "the farms on the sandy rise / the homesteads by the dune."** This fits
the geography precisely: the eastern **Meierij van 's-Hertogenbosch** (Uden–Boekel–Gemert)
sits on the poor, sandy heathlands of the **Peel** region, where farmsteads clustered on
the higher, drier sandy grounds. Toponymic farm-names of the *X-hoven* type are common
across this landscape (compare Eindhoven, Waalhoven, etc.).

**Is there a place literally called Duijnhoven?** There is **no surviving town/village**
of that name today. Like most *-hoven* surnames in Brabant, it derives from a **local
farm or field-name (veldnaam / boerderijnaam)** rather than a still-existing settlement —
a hallmark of the process by which East-Brabant farm names became hereditary surnames.
Such micro-toponyms often vanished from the map once the surname detached from the
specific holding. (The name is still concentrated in the Oss / Uden / Land van Cuijk
area of Noord-Brabant today, consistent with a local Meierij origin.)

**Formation timeline:** hereditary surnames were not universal in the Netherlands until
Napoleon's **1811** civil-registration decree, but Catholic Brabant families in the
Meierij were already using stable toponymic surnames like *van Duijnhoven* in the parish
registers **well before 1811** — as the 1750s–1790s Uden entries above demonstrate.
