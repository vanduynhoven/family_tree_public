Research compiled 2026-08-28. Covers the family's arrival in the United States (Nov 1950),
naturalization (1956), the four US-born children (1951–1956), and the record sets / access
paths for each. Web search reached the *record collection guides* but not the indexed record
detail behind login (Ancestry / FamilySearch), so this file records **which collection holds

## 1. 1950 US Census — NOT APPLICABLE

The 1950 US Census was enumerated **1 April 1950**. The family did not arrive until **November 1950**,
so they are **absent from the 1950 US Census** — correct expectation, no record to find on the US side.

- On 1 Apr 1950 they were still in the Netherlands (a Dutch civil register / *bevolkingsregister*
  entry for Boekel/Uden/Bakel would be the equivalent snapshot — pursue via BHIC, not US census).
- **1960 US Census**: restricted under the 72-year rule until **April 2032**. Nothing accessible now.
- **Minnesota state census**: Minnesota's last decennial *state* census was **1905**; there is **no
  1950s Minnesota state census**. So no state-census record exists for this family either.

**Conclusion:** No US or MN census will place this family before 2032 (1960 release). Use immigration,
naturalization, church, and vital records instead.

## 2. Immigration — Passenger Manifest, November 1950

### Route (reconstructed)
Rotterdam → **Hook of Holland** → (North Sea ferry) → **Harwich, England** → boat-train →
**Southampton** → transatlantic liner → **New York**. This is the standard 1950 Dutch-emigrant
routing and matches the family's recorded waypoints (visa Rotterdam 8 Nov 1950; **Harwich 29 Nov /
Southampton 30 Nov 1950**).

### Ship — most likely RMS *Queen Elizabeth* (Cunard), Southampton sailing ~30 Nov / 1 Dec 1950
- Cunard ran the weekly Southampton–Cherbourg–New York express service with *Queen Elizabeth* and
  *Queen Mary*. A **30 Nov 1950 Southampton departure arrives New York ~5 Dec 1950** (≈5-day crossing).
- ⚠️ The often-cited GG Archives list "**QE 16 November 1950**"
  (https://www.ggarchives.com/OT/PLs/Cunard/QueenElizabeth-PassengerList-1950-11-16.html)
  **does NOT match** — that sailing left two weeks *before* the family reached Southampton, and it is a
  **Cabin-Class souvenir list only**. Dutch emigrant families traveled **Tourist/Third class**, which
  never appears on those souvenir lists regardless. Do not treat the 16 Nov list as their voyage.
- The actual manifest is the **official INS arrival manifest at New York**, not the souvenir list.

### Where the real manifest lives (action items)
1. **NARA** — *Passenger and Crew Lists of Vessels Arriving at New York, 1897–1957*
   (NARA microfilm **A3461 / T715 continuation**). Coverage runs to Dec 1957, so Dec 1950 is included.
   - https://www.archives.gov/research/immigration/passenger-arrival.html
2. **Ancestry.com** — *New York, U.S., Arriving Passenger and Crew Lists, 1820–1957* (indexed, imaged).
   Search: surname `Van Duynhoven` **AND** `Van Duijnhoven`, first names Johan/Johannes and
   Anna/Anna Maria, arrival year **1950**, port New York. This is the fastest path to the image.
3. **FamilySearch** — same NY passenger-list collection is browsable/indexed free with a login.
4. **Confirm the exact ship + date** once the manifest is found; then cross-check the Cunard sailing
   calendar for that week to lock the vessel name.

### Dutch-side departure record (independent confirmation)
- **Open Archieven** indexes Dutch emigration/departure records (e.g. Rotterdam *vertrek* lists).
  Search the free JSON API for a 1950 Rotterdam departure:
  `https://api.openarchieven.nl/1.0/records/search.json?name=van%20Duijnhoven&number_show=20`
  (a sample 1955 Rotterdam→N.America departure via SS *Waterman* confirms this record type exists).

## 3. Naturalization — 5 June 1956, Mankato, MN

Mankato is in **Blue Earth County**. Naturalizations in this era were handled by the **US District
Court** (Minnesota, likely the **4th/5th Division**) rather than the county court — the certificate
numbers (7-digit, INS-issued: Johan **7468861**, Anna **7468860/…862**) confirm a federal proceeding.

### Where the records live (action items)
1. **USCIS Genealogy Program** — the authoritative source for a **C-File** (Certificate File) tied to a
   post-1906 certificate number. This is the richest record: petition, declaration of intention, photos,
   visa/immigration references, and the **arrival ship + date** (which will confirm §2 above).
   - Index search **G-1041** ($65), then records request **G-1041A** ($65/file), ~8–12 weeks.
   - https://www.uscis.gov/records/genealogy
   - Provide both certificate numbers and the 5 Jun 1956 Mankato date to speed the match.
2. **NARA (Chicago)** — holds US District Court of Minnesota naturalization files; also the source of the
   *Minnesota Naturalization Card Index 1930–1988* digitized on FamilySearch
   (https://www.familysearch.org/wiki/en/Minnesota_Naturalization_Card_Index_-_FamilySearch_Historical_Records —
   the index card gives petition #, court, and date; the card is the fast confirmation).
3. **Minnesota Historical Society (MNHS)** — holds naturalization records/indexes for all 87 counties
   plus the MN Supreme Court, searchable via the Iron Range Research Center index; MNHS can provide
   copies (Official Use option). https://libguides.mnhs.org/naturalization
4. **Ancestry** — *Minnesota, U.S., Naturalization Records Index* and the federal naturalization
   collections; search `Van Duynhoven`/`Van Duijnhoven`, 1956, Minnesota.

### Value note
The **C-File is the single highest-value US record** for this family: it typically embeds the visa
application, sponsor/affidavit, arrival manifest reference, and photographs — resolving the ship,
exact arrival date, and the certificate-number discrepancy in one file.

All four were **born in Minnesota** and **baptized at St. Brendan's Catholic Church, Green Isle**
(Sibley County). Births in this era were filed **at the county of birth** and rolled up into the

> Note: "Arlington" (the family's noted birthplace town) is in **Sibley County**, not Blue Earth.
> Blue Earth County (Mankato) is where the **naturalization** happened, not the births. Search
> **Sibley County first**, then McLeod as a fallback.

### Where the records live (action items)
1. **Minnesota Official Marriage/Birth records via MDH** — the MN Dept. of Health Office of Vital
   Records issues certified birth certificates (statewide system; any county office can serve any
   record). https://www.health.state.mn.us/people/vitalrecords/index.html — 651-201-5970.
   Births < 100 years old are **non-public**; certified copies require proving a tangible interest
   (Arthur, as a direct descendant of the parents, qualifies for the parents'/uncles' records via
   lineage).
2. **Minnesota Birth Index 1935–2002** (Ancestry-provided, mirrored on FamilySearch) — a *public index*
   that will confirm each birth's date, county, and mother's maiden name (Cornelissen) without the
   restriction. https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Minnesota,_Birth_Index_-_FamilySearch_Historical_Records
   Search `Vanduynhoven` / `Van Duynhoven` / `Van Duijnhoven`, 1951–1956, mother surname Cornelissen.
3. **MNHS county birth registers** — Sibley County birth records (1857–1973) held at MNHS; viewable
   on microfilm / in person. https://libguides.mnhs.org/vital/birth
4. **St. Brendan's baptismal records** — parish **closed 2021**; records transferred to the
   **Diocese of New Ulm** archives. Baptismal registers give birth + baptism dates, parents, and
   godparents (often relatives — useful for the wider tree). Request via the Diocese of New Ulm.
