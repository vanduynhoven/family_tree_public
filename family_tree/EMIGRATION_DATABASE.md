# Van Duynhoven / van Duijnhoven / Van Dyn Hoven — Emigration Database (Netherlands → USA)

*Compiled 2026-08-28. A comprehensive record of documented emigration waves from the Netherlands to the United States for all spelling variants of the surname. Sources are cited inline; where a fact is inferred rather than documented in a primary record, it is marked **(inferred)**.*

## Surname variants tracked

| Variant | Predominant use | Region of origin |
|---|---|---|
| van Duijnhoven | Dutch civil registry (ij digraph) | Noord-Brabant (Uden/Boekel/Schaijk area) |
| van Duynhoven / Van Duynhoven | US East Coast (NY) | Noord-Holland (Amsterdam / Halfweg) |
| Van Dyn Hoven / Vandynhoven | US Wisconsin (Little Chute / Kaukauna) | Noord-Brabant (Uden area, via 1848+ migration) |

**Key structural finding:** the three US clusters are almost certainly *not* one family. They descend from at least two — probably three — separate Dutch origin points and three distinct emigration events spread across ~100 years. Confirming or refuting a shared common ancestor requires Dutch parish/civil records for each origin point (see "Open questions" at the end).

## WAVE 3 — "Van Dyn Hoven" of Little Chute / Kaukauna, Wisconsin (19th-century Uden wave)

**Status: CONFIRMED as a distinct, well-established branch; founding immigrant not yet individually identified.**

Little Chute, Wisconsin was founded in **1848** by a group of Dutch Catholics from **Uden, Noord-Brabant**, led by the Dominican missionary **Father Theodore J. van den Broek** (himself from Uden). This began a chain migration of Uden-area Catholics to northeast Wisconsin that continued into the early 20th century. Because Johan's Brabant line is *also* from Uden, this branch is the **most plausible distant relative** of the Minnesota line — but a link is unproven and would predate the 1850s.

**Origin in NL:** Uden area, **Noord-Brabant** (via the 1848+ van den Broek migration wave). Individual emigrant, ship, and arrival date **not yet identified** — likely mid-to-late 19th century, arriving via New York, Boston, or Quebec then inland to Wisconsin.

The Stevens Point Area Genealogy Society and UW–Madison "Dutch in Wisconsin" project hold context material.

## Open questions / next research steps

1. **Are the three US branches related?** The MN (Brabant/Uden) and WI (Brabant/Uden) branches share an origin *region* and could share an 18th-c. or earlier common ancestor; the Warwick NY branch (North Holland) is a separate family that happens to share the surname. Proving/disproving links requires pushing each line back through Dutch civil (post-1811) and parish (pre-1811) records to a shared couple — none found yet.
2. **Locate the ship manifests** for Wave 1 (Nov 1950 NY arrival) and Wave 2 (1953–54 NY arrival) via NARA T715 / statueofliberty.org / stevemorse.org one-step forms.
3. **Identify the founding Wisconsin emigrant** (mid-19th c.) — St. John Nepomucene parish records + Outagamie County naturalizations + Uden Landverhuizers registers.
4. **Order USCIS A-Files** for Johan & Anna (visa apps, sponsor affidavits, photos) via the Genealogy Program — see `../RECORDS_REQUEST_GUIDE.md`.
5. **Trace the Amsterdam van Duijnhoven line** (John Sr. × Marie Tol) in the Amsterdam/Haarlemmermeer civil registry to establish the Warwick branch's deeper Dutch ancestry.
