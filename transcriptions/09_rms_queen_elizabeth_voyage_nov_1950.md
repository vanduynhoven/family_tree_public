## Voyage Details

### Ship Information
- **Ship Name**: RMS Queen Elizabeth
- **Steamship Line**: Cunard Line
- **Tonnage**: 83,673 gross tons — World's largest liner at the time
- **Capacity**: 2,314 passengers (850 first class, 720 cabin class, 744 tourist class)

### Voyage Information
- **Departure**: November 16, 1950 from Southampton, England
- **Via**: Cherbourg, France
- **Arrival**: ~November 20-21, 1950 in New York, USA
- **Commander**: Commodore G. E. Cove
- **Crossing Time**: Approximately 4-5 days

### Note on Date
The handwritten date "29 November 1950" likely refers to when they reached their final destination in Minnesota (Green Isle), not the ship arrival date. The Queen Elizabeth typically made the crossing in about 4-5 days.

**Timeline Reconstruction**:
- Nov 16, 1950: Departed Southampton on RMS Queen Elizabeth
- Nov 20-21, 1950: Arrived New York Harbor
- Nov 29, 1950: Arrived in Green Isle, Minnesota (by train)

## Passengers
- **Johan van Duijnhoven** (b. 1915, Uden) — listed as "John"
- **Anna Maria Cornelissen** (b. 1916, Bakel)

They traveled as newlyweds, having married on October 5, 1950 in Bakel, just 6 weeks before departure.

## Historical Context

The RMS Queen Elizabeth was the largest ocean liner ever built at that time. Along with her sister ship RMS Queen Mary, she provided weekly transatlantic service between Southampton and New York. The ship was launched in 1938 but spent WWII as a troopship before entering civilian service in 1946.

For Dutch emigrants like Johan and Anna Maria, the typical route was:
1. Train from Noord-Brabant to Rotterdam or Hook of Holland
2. Ferry across the English Channel to Harwich or Southampton
3. Board the transatlantic liner at Southampton
4. Stop at Cherbourg, France to pick up additional passengers
5. Cross the Atlantic (4-5 days)
6. Arrive at Pier 90 in New York
7. Process through immigration
8. Board train to final destination (Minnesota)
