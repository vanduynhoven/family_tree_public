# Van Duynhoven Immigration Timeline
## From Noord-Brabant, Netherlands to Minnesota, USA (1949-1956)

*A chronological account of Johan and Anna van Duynhoven's emigration journey*

---

## Pre-Emigration Period (1949-1950)

### December 27, 1949
**📍 's-Hertogenbosch, Netherlands**
- Johan van Duynhoven obtains his Dutch passport
- Issued by: Commissaris der Koningin in de Provincie Noord-Brabant (Commissioner of the Queen in the Province of North Brabant)
- Valid for: All countries of the world
- Expiration: December 26, 1951

### October 5, 1950
**📍 Bakel en Milheeze, Noord-Brabant**
- **Civil Marriage Ceremony**
- Johan van Duynhoven (age 34) marries Anna Maria Cornelissen (age 34)
- Town: Bakel en Milheeze
- County: Noord-Brabant

### November 8, 1950
**📍 Rotterdam, Netherlands**
- **U.S. Immigration Visa Issued**
- Visa Type: Nonquota Immigration Visa
- Visa Number: 2/6
- Issued to: Johan van Duynhoven
- Issued by: Violet Smith, American Vice Consul
- Location: American Vice Consulate, Rotterdam

### November 22, 1950
**📍 's-Gravenhage (The Hague), Netherlands**
- **Exit from the Netherlands**
- Departure stamp in passport
- Leaving their homeland for America

### November 28, 1950
**📍 Milheeze, Noord-Brabant**
- **Church Marriage Ceremony** (3 weeks after civil ceremony, 6 days after departure stamp)
- *Note: The church wedding date appears to be after the exit stamp, suggesting either:*
  - *The church ceremony was performed earlier and recorded later*
  - *A brief return for the religious ceremony*
  - *A transcription discrepancy in the documents*
- Church: Heilige Willibrord (Holy Willibrord)
- Diocese: Buscodue ('s-Hertogenbosch)
- Officiant: Father Puchomius
- Witnesses: Johannes Cornelissen and Genardus van Duijnhoven

## The Journey (November 1950)

### November 29, 1950
**📍 Harwich, England**
- **Arrival in United Kingdom**
- Immigration Officer stamp
- Route: Likely arrived by ferry from Hook of Holland

### November 30, 1950
**📍 Southampton, England**
- **Departure for America**
- Immigration Officer stamp
- Southampton was a major transatlantic liner port
- Beginning of ocean voyage to the United States

### Late November / Early December 1950
**🚢 Atlantic Ocean**
- Transatlantic crossing
- Typical voyage duration: 5-7 days
- Estimated arrival in USA: Early December 1950

---

## Settlement in America (1950-1956)

### December 1950
**📍 Green Isle, Minnesota, USA**
- Johan and Anna arrive and settle in Green Isle
- Located in Sibley County, Minnesota
- Farming community with significant German-Catholic population
- Join St. Brendan's Catholic Church parish

### December 8, 1951
**📍 Arlington, Minnesota**
- **First American-born child**
- Morris Wilhelm Peter van Duynhoven born
- Baptized December 16, 1951 at St. Brendan's Catholic Church, Green Isle

### March 5, 1953
**📍 Arlington, Minnesota**
- **Second child born**
- Wilhelmus Josephus van Duynhoven
- Baptized March 15, 1953 at St. Brendan's Catholic Church, Green Isle

### December 19, 1954
**📍 Arlington, Minnesota**
- **Third child born**
- Anna Maria van Duynhoven
- Baptized December 27, 1954 at St. Brendan's Catholic Church, Green Isle

### April 24, 1956
**📍 Arlington, Minnesota**
- **Fourth child born**
- Peter John van Duynhoven
- Baptized May 6, 1956 at St. Brendan's Catholic Church, Green Isle

### June 5, 1956
**📍 Mankato, Minnesota**
- **NATURALIZATION - CITIZENSHIP GRANTED**
- United States District Court, District of Minnesota, Second Division
- Both Johan and Anna become U.S. citizens on the same day

**Johan van Duynhoven:**
- Certificate No.: 7468861
- Petition No.: 1109

**Anna van Duynhoven:**
- Certificate No.: 7468862
- Petition No.: 1110

- Clerk: Chell M. Smith
- Deputy Clerk: Mamie A. Bredeson

## Historical Context

### Why Minnesota?

In the post-WWII period (1945-1960), many Dutch emigrants chose Minnesota for several reasons:
- Established Dutch-American communities
- Similar agricultural landscape to the Netherlands
- Strong Catholic parishes (especially German-Catholic communities)
- Opportunities in farming and dairy

### Post-War Dutch Emigration

After World War II, the Netherlands experienced significant emigration due to:
- Housing shortages
- Limited economic opportunities
- Government-sponsored emigration programs
- Desire for new opportunities in America, Canada, and Australia

Johan and Anna were part of this wave, leaving the Netherlands just five years after the end of WWII.
