## Legal Analysis

### 1. Were the Children Born Dutch Nationals?

**YES.** Under Dutch law (Rijkswet op het Nederlanderschap), Dutch nationality is acquired primarily through *jus sanguinis* (right of blood), not *jus soli* (right of birthplace).

**Artikel 3(1) RWN (1985) states:**
> "Nederlander is het kind waarvan ten tijde van zijn geboorte de vader of de moeder Nederlander is"
> (A child whose father or mother is a Dutch national at the time of birth is a Dutch national)

Since Johan van Duynhoven was a Dutch national at the time of each child's birth (1951-1956), all four children were **born Dutch nationals**, regardless of being born in the United States.

**This means all four children held dual nationality at birth:**
- Dutch nationality (through father Johan)
- U.S. nationality (through birth in the United States - *jus soli*)

---

### 2. What Happened When the Parents Naturalized U.S. in 1956?

**The children LOST their Dutch nationality.**

The Wet op het Nederlanderschap en het Ingezetenschap (WNI) of 1892 was in effect until December 31, 1984. Under this law:

**Artikel 7(1) WNI (1892) stated:**
> "Nederlanderschap wordt verloren door naturalisatie in een ander land of, voor zoveel een minderjarige betreft, door het deelachtig worden van een andere nationaliteit door de naturalisatie in een ander land hetzij van de vader, hetzij van de moeder..."

**Translation:** "Dutch nationality is lost by naturalization in another country or, as far as a minor is concerned, by sharing in the acquisition of another nationality through the naturalization of the father or mother in another country..."

**Key Points:**
1. When Johan and Anna naturalized as U.S. citizens on June 5, 1956, they **voluntarily lost their Dutch nationality**
2. All four minor children **automatically lost their Dutch nationality** by sharing in their parents' naturalization
3. The children did not become stateless (they retained U.S. citizenship by birth)
4. No formal renunciation was required - the loss was **automatic by operation of law** ("van rechtswege")

---

### 3. Could the Children Have Retained Dutch Nationality?

**NO, not under the 1892 law.** 

Under the WNI (1892), there was no provision allowing minor children to retain Dutch nationality when both parents naturalized to another country. The principle of "unity of nationality within the family" (*eenheid van de Nederlandse nationaliteit van het gezin*) meant minor children followed the nationality of their parents.

**Exception that did NOT apply:**
If only one parent had naturalized and the other parent remained Dutch, the children could have retained Dutch nationality through the remaining Dutch parent. However, both Johan AND Anna naturalized on the same day (June 5, 1956), so this exception did not apply.

---

### 4. Were There Options to Restore Dutch Nationality?

**YES, but the windows have closed.**

The Rijkswet op het Nederlanderschap (RWN) that replaced the WNI (1892) on January 1, 1985, and subsequent revisions, created several temporary option procedures:

#### Option Period 1: February 1, 2001 - March 31, 2003
For former Dutch nationals who lost nationality under Artikel 15c RWN (1985) and were issued a Dutch document after January 1, 1990.
- **NOT APPLICABLE** - The children lost nationality in 1956, not under this provision.

#### Option Period 2: April 1, 2003 - March 31, 2005 ("Stage 2")
For former Dutch nationals who:
- Lost Dutch nationality under the former Artikel 15c RWN
- Had NOT been issued a Dutch document after January 1, 1990
- **NOT APPLICABLE** - Again, this applied to those who lost nationality under a different provision.

#### Option Period 3: April 1, 2003 - March 31, 2013 ("Stage 3")
For former Dutch nationals who lost Dutch nationality **as adults prior to April 1, 2003** because they acquired a foreign nationality:
- Who were born in the country of the acquired nationality and were living there, OR
- Who lived for at least 5 uninterrupted years as a minor in that country, OR
- Who were married to a national of that country when acquiring it

**POTENTIALLY APPLICABLE** - The Van Duynhoven children:
- Were born in the USA (the country of naturalization)
- Had lived for at least 5 years as minors in the USA

**However:**
- This option applied to those who "lost Dutch nationality **as adults**" - the children lost nationality as minors
- This option period closed on **March 31, 2013**
- Even if it had applied, the window has been closed for over 13 years

---

### 5. Current Options (2026)

#### Option A: Re-acquisition by Option Statement (Artikel 6(1)f RWN)

A former Dutch national can re-acquire Dutch nationality by:
1. Obtaining a **long-term visa for a non-temporary purpose** to reside in the Kingdom of the Netherlands
2. Residing there for **at least one (1) uninterrupted year**
3. Submitting an option statement at their municipality
4. They may retain their other nationalities (including U.S.)

**Requirements:**
- Must be able to obtain a long-term, non-temporary residence permit
- Must have lawful residence for at least one full year
- The option is available to any former Dutch national

**Challenges:**
- The children (now ages ~70-75 in 2026) are not eligible for the automatic "Wedertoelating" (Re-admission) visa because they were not **both** born AND raised in the Netherlands
- They would need to qualify for another type of long-term visa (employment, family reunification, etc.)

#### Option B: Naturalization

- Requires residence in the Netherlands (or a third country, not the USA)
- More stringent requirements than option procedure
- Takes longer
- Generally requires integration exam and Dutch language proficiency

**Important:** Neither option nor naturalization is possible while residing in the USA (the country of current nationality). This is against Dutch public policy.

---

### 6. What About Future Generations?

**The children's own children and grandchildren:**

Since the four Van Duynhoven children lost Dutch nationality in 1956 and never re-acquired it:
- Their children were **NOT born Dutch nationals**
- Their grandchildren were **NOT born Dutch nationals**
- Dutch nationality cannot be transmitted if the parent is not a Dutch national at the time of birth

**The chain of Dutch nationality was broken in 1956.**

## Legal References

### Wet op het Nederlanderschap en het Ingezetenschap (WNI) 1892
- **Artikel 7(1):** Loss of Dutch nationality through naturalization in another country, including minor children

### Rijkswet op het Nederlanderschap (RWN) 1985
- **Artikel 3(1):** Acquisition of Dutch nationality at birth through Dutch parent
- **Artikel 6(1)f:** Re-acquisition by option statement for former Dutch nationals after 1 year residence
- **Artikel 15(1)a:** Loss by voluntary acquisition of foreign nationality (with exceptions since 2003)
- **Artikel 15(1)c:** Loss by residence abroad for 13 years (formerly 10 years) without renewal
- **Artikel 16:** Loss for minors when parent loses nationality

The four children of Johan and Anna Van Duynhoven:

   - Residing there for at least one uninterrupted year

6. **Cannot transmit Dutch nationality** to their own children or grandchildren (the chain was broken in 1956)
