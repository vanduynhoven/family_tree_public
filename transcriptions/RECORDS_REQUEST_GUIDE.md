## Option 1: USCIS Genealogy Program (Recommended)

The U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services operates a dedicated genealogy program specifically designed for family history researchers. This is the **preferred method** for most historical immigration records.

### What Records Are Available

- **Naturalization Certificate Files (C-Files)** — The primary case file created when someone became a citizen
- **Alien Registration Forms** — Registration cards from the 1940s
- **Visa Files** — Original visa applications and supporting documents
- **Registry Files** — Records for immigrants who entered without inspection

### Step-by-Step Process

#### Step 1: Create a USCIS Online Account

1. Go to: **https://www.uscis.gov/records/genealogy**
2. Click "Create an Account" if you don't have one
3. Complete the registration with your email and personal information

#### Step 2: Submit an Index Search Request (Form G-1041)

This first step searches the USCIS database to find out what records exist for your subjects.

**Fee:** $65 (non-refundable)

**Information to provide:**

```
Date of Birth:          November 12, 1915
Place of Birth:         Uden, Noord-Brabant, Netherlands
Date of Naturalization: June 5, 1956
Certificate Number:     7468861
Port of Entry:          New York
Date of Entry:          November 23, 1950

Maiden Name:            Cornelissen

                        (Note: Some records show 1918 - discrepancy)
Place of Birth:         Netherlands

```

#### Step 3: Receive Index Search Results

USCIS will send you a response indicating:
- Whether records were found
- What type of records exist
- File numbers for each record

**Timeline:** 4-8 weeks

#### Step 4: Request Copies of Records (Form G-1041A)

If records are found, submit a records request:

**Fee:** $65 per file

You'll receive photocopies of the complete file contents.

**Timeline:** 4-8 additional weeks

### What You'll Likely Receive

**Naturalization C-File typically contains:**
- Declaration of Intention ("First Papers")
- Petition for Naturalization
- Certificate of Arrival
- Biographical information questionnaire
- Witness affidavits (neighbors/employers who vouched for them)
- Oath of Allegiance record
- Photographs (sometimes)
- Court correspondence

## Option 2: FOIA Request to USCIS

Use this method if the Genealogy Program doesn't have the records you need, or if you prefer to file under the Freedom of Information Act.

### How to Submit

**Online (Fastest):**
https://www.uscis.gov/records/request-records-through-the-freedom-of-information-act-or-privacy-act

**By Mail:**
```
USCIS FOIA/PA Office
National Records Center
P.O. Box 648010
Lee's Summit, MO 64064-8010
```

**By Email:**
uscis.foia@uscis.dhs.gov

### FOIA Fees

- **Search fees:** Usually waived for genealogical requests
- **Duplication fees:** $0.10-0.25 per page
- **Most genealogy requests:** Free or under $25
- **Complex requests:** May be $50-100

### FOIA Timeline

- **Acknowledgment:** 1-2 weeks
- **Response:** 20 business days (legally required, but often longer)
- **Actual receipt:** 2-6 months typical due to backlogs

## What These Files Typically Contain

### Naturalization C-File (Most Valuable)

| Document | What It Shows |
|----------|---------------|
| Declaration of Intention | First step toward citizenship; personal details, occupation |
| Petition for Naturalization | Formal application with biographical data |
| Certificate of Arrival | Proof of legal entry to the U.S. |
| Biographical Form | Detailed personal history questionnaire |
| Witness Affidavits | Statements from people who knew them |
| Photographs | Portrait photos (not always included) |
| Oath of Allegiance | Record of the citizenship ceremony |
| Correspondence | Letters between the applicant and government |

### Visa File (If Available)

| Document | What It Shows |
|----------|---------------|
| Visa Application | Completed in the Netherlands before departure |
| Sponsor Affidavit | Who in the U.S. vouched for them financially |
| Medical Examination | Health screening results |
| Police Certificate | Criminal background check from Netherlands |
| Financial Statements | Proof of support/employment prospects |
| Photographs | Passport-style photos |
| Letters of Support | From family or employers in U.S. |

### Alien Registration File (If It Exists)

| Document | What It Shows |
|----------|---------------|
| AR-2 Form | Registration card from 1940 Alien Registration Act |
| Fingerprint Card | Physical fingerprints taken at registration |
| Address History | All U.S. addresses |
| Employment History | Jobs held in the U.S. |
| Updates | Any subsequent address changes reported |

## Recommended Strategy

### Step 1: Start with Free Resources
Search FamilySearch.org for the ship manifest — this costs nothing and may give you the ship name and travel details immediately.

### Step 2: Submit USCIS Genealogy Index Search
Pay the $65 to find out exactly what records exist. This is the most efficient path.

### Step 3: Order the C-Files
Once you know what's available, order copies of the naturalization case files ($65 each). These are typically the most comprehensive.

### Step 4: FOIA as Backup
If the genealogy program doesn't have certain records, file a FOIA request for anything missing.

## Important Notes

### Spelling Variations
Always search for multiple spellings:
- van Duynhoven
- van Duijnhoven  
- Van Duynhoven
- Vanduynhoven
- Duynhoven

### The Birth Year Discrepancy
Anna Maria's birth year appears as both **1916** (correct) and **1918** (incorrect) in different documents. Mention both dates in your request to ensure records aren't missed.

### Privacy Considerations
Immigration records for deceased individuals are generally available. You may need to provide:
- Proof of death (if available)
- Proof of relationship (for recent records)
- Your identification

### Processing Delays
Both USCIS Genealogy and FOIA requests can experience significant backlogs. Plan for:
- 2-3 months minimum
- 6+ months in some cases
- Check status online periodically
